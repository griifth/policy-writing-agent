# Source Assessment

Use this file before making substantive claims about a policy research book.

## Coverage Ladder

### `A` Full Or Near-Complete Text

Examples:

- full PDF
- OCR-clean TXT
- EPUB export
- scanned book with usable OCR

Allowed:

- infer stable explicit method
- infer hidden thinking model
- build prompt, comparison matrix, or skill seed

### `B` Several Substantial Chapters

Examples:

- introduction plus theory plus methods plus conclusion
- several long excerpts across the book

Allowed:

- extract likely framework
- infer parts of the thinking model
- produce medium-confidence distillation

Watch for:

- chapter selection bias
- over-weighting the introduction

### `C` Preface Plus TOC Plus Conclusion

Examples:

- publisher preview
- limited Google Books view
- TOC plus introduction

Allowed:

- identify probable architecture
- extract explicit framing language
- propose tentative analytical model

Not allowed:

- strong claims about detailed method
- strong claims about omissions or author bias unless repeated evidence is visible

### `D` Metadata Or Review Only

Examples:

- catalog page
- short review
- blog introduction

Allowed:

- topic discovery
- likely orientation
- low-confidence structural guess

Not allowed:

- real hidden-model distillation

## Source Form Checks

Adjust expectations by genre:

- `single-author research book`: strongest basis for stable method inference
- `edited volume`: separate editor framing from contributor logic
- `practitioner handbook`: expect stronger tool orientation and weaker theory
- `ideological or advocacy text`: separate normative force from analytical method
- `case monograph`: expect high depth but lower transferability

## Evidence Hierarchy

Prefer evidence in this order:

1. author’s own full text
2. preface, introduction, chapter openings, conclusion
3. diagrams, tables, summary boxes
4. repeated wording across chapters
5. interviews or reviews by the author
6. publisher copy or catalog metadata

When weaker evidence is doing most of the work, say so directly.

## Confidence Rule

Start from coverage level, then adjust:

- raise confidence when multiple chapters repeat the same analytical sequence
- lower confidence when the book is fragmentary, multi-author, or internally inconsistent
- lower confidence when claims depend on one memorable sentence

## Failure Modes

Avoid these errors:

- mistaking topic labels for method
- treating chapter titles as proof of causal logic
- forcing every book into a generic policy-cycle frame
- confusing normative preference with analytical method
- inferring deep author intent from marketing copy

## Minimum Disclosure In Output

Always state:

- what materials were actually seen
- the coverage level used
- whether the result is full, partial, or provisional
